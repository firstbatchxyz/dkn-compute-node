# DKN × leyten/shard: Adoption Brief

## 1. VOCAB VERDICT — Your tokenizer warning was WRONG (for this design)

**GLM-4-9B CAN draft GLM-5.2. Unambiguously. The MTP head is not required.**

Your brief's warning conflated two different numbers:

- **154880 / 151552 are `config.vocab_size` — the padded weight-matrix row count** of the embedding/lm_head. GLM pads these up to a hardware-friendly multiple. This is what `glm_draft_bench.py:3` prints ("GLM-5.2(154880) vs GLM-4-9B(151552)"), and it is *not* the usable token-id space.
- **The tokenizer's real id range is identical over all text both models actually emit.** `glm_draft_compat.py` exists specifically to verify this: it checks encode-equality (same string → same id sequence) AND decode-equality (same id → same piece) in both directions, producing the literal verdict string `"COMPATIBLE — usable as a drop-in draft"`.

Why the gap is irrelevant **by construction**, not by luck:

1. **Acceptance is exact integer token-id equality against the target's own greedy argmax** — `glm_swarm_nvfp4_draft.py:57-75`: `for j in range(K): if drafts[j] == r[j]: n += 1; else: break`. `r[j]` is GLM-5.2's own `lm_head.argmax(-1)`. There is **no** logit comparison, no distribution-ratio sampling, no vocab intersection, no id remapping anywhere in the codebase. A vocab divergence can therefore only ever cost **speed** (acceptance rate `g → 0`, falling back to 1 tok/round) — it can **never** corrupt output. The emitted stream is provably bit-identical to plain greedy decode of GLM-5.2 (`"tokens_match": true`, matching SHA256 in the receipt).
2. **The one real concern — feeding a target-only id (≥151552) into the draft's embedding — is handled by a clamp**, not by the acceptance path: `glm_swarm_nvfp4_cg.py` `dstep` uses `t if t < DVOCAB else 0`. And in practice it never fires: GLM-5.2's actual outputs top out at id 98668 in the receipt, deep inside the shared low-id region. The `glm_draft_bench.py:41-46` "must clamp" crash probe deliberately feeds an out-of-range id to *prove* the embedding gap exists — it is an input-embedding concern, not an acceptance concern.

**Bottom line: your original plan — serve GLM-5.2 pipeline-parallel with a GLM-4-9B drafter — is valid as-is.** Greedy/argmax spec-decode over raw token-ids sidesteps the vocab question entirely. Drop the MTP-head workaround from the design; it was solving a non-problem. (MTP remains a *fallback* lever if measured acceptance `g` with the 9B draft turns out poor — but the 9B is the deeper depth-K replacement for MTP's depth-1, per `glm_swarm_nvfp4_draft.py:2`, and is the path they actually run at 30 tok/s.)

One caveat that survives: this guarantee holds **for greedy decode only**. The moment DKN wants temperature/top-p sampling end-to-end, you need true speculative sampling (probability-ratio acceptance), which *does* require aligned distributions over a shared vocab — and there the padded-width gap and per-engine logit differences come back. Shard ships greedy-only and leans on that for its correctness oracle. Plan your sampling story accordingly.

## 2. ADOPT vs PORT — Port the techniques; do not adopt the engine

**The substrate mismatch is fatal to wholesale adoption, and it's worse than "different language."** Shard's two tiers have *opposite* portability:

| Layer | What it is | Ports to DKN? |
|---|---|---|
| Transport + pipeline coordinator | raw TCP sockets, embed/norm/lm_head by hand, hidden-state forwarding, spec-decode loop with depth-N pipelining | **Yes — as a design.** Pure Python/torch/safetensors, nothing vLLM-bound. |
| Compute kernels | NVFP4 MoE via vLLM `FusedMoE` + `ModelOptNvFp4Config` + CUTLASS GEMM; GLM-5.2 on vLLM's patched `deepseek_v2.py` MLA path; `torch.compile` + CUDA-graph draft | **No.** CUDA/Blackwell/vLLM-bound. **No GGUF equivalent for NVFP4 exists.** |

The kernel tier is the killer. NVFP4 execution requires vLLM's `process_weights_after_loading` swizzle, `init_workspace_manager`, `set_forward_context`, and a CUTLASS NVFP4 GEMM — and the GLM-5.2 target runs through vLLM's `deepseek_v2.py` with a **source-level monkeypatch** (`patch_vllm_dense.py`) just to run on sm_120. None of this maps to llama.cpp. GGUF has **no NVFP4 path at all**; the nearest is Q4 K-quants with a completely different scale layout and accuracy profile. The 30 tok/s GLM-5.2-744B number is *manufactured by the NVFP4/CUTLASS kernels on RTX PRO 6000 (Blackwell)* — it does not transfer to a GGUF cut.

**The three options, scored:**

- **(a) Adopt wholesale as a parallel PyTorch serving path on a GPU-subset of DKN nodes.** This means running a *second engine* — vLLM + torch + CUTLASS, CUDA-only, 48GB+ Blackwell-class nodes — alongside the llama.cpp fleet. Reach collapses: you lose ROCm, Metal, and every node that isn't a recent high-VRAM NVIDIA card. You'd be operating a competitor's full stack, inheriting their unfinished pieces (NAT hole-punching is a stub, M7 trustless verification is "months not weeks," the CG-draft + rollback path is *admitted not yet bit-clean* — `g` collapses 1.94→0.3, "cudagraph-speed XOR clean-rollback"). You'd fork their bugs. **Reject** as the primary path.
- **(b) Port the techniques into the Rust/llama.cpp node.** You keep DKN's heterogeneous reach, your Rust QUIC transport (which is *ahead* of theirs — see §3), your validation/trust/metering rails. You re-implement the algorithmic wins against llama.cpp's own graph/KV API. Higher up-front dev cost on the CUDA-graph-draft + static-KV-rollback piece, but everything you build serves the whole fleet. **This is the recommendation.**
- **(c) Hybrid.** Port the portable tier now (§3); keep option (a) on the table as an **optional, opt-in, NVIDIA-only "frontier lane"** *if and only if* you decide DKN must serve 744B-class NVFP4 models that GGUF genuinely cannot. Frontier MoE at NVFP4 is the one thing llama.cpp cannot match — if that workload is a business requirement, a small vLLM-backed sub-swarm is the honest answer, run as a separate node type behind the same router/metering. But don't lead with it.

**Recommendation: (b), with (c) held as a contingency for NVFP4-only frontier models.** Rationale: DKN's entire value proposition is ~20k *heterogeneous* nodes. Shard's engine is a ~16-node *homogeneous Blackwell* design. Adopting it inverts your moat. Port the ideas, keep the substrate, and treat a vLLM frontier lane as a deliberate separate product decision, not a default.

## 3. TECHNIQUES TO LIFT — Concrete, with portability and difficulty

Ranked by value-to-effort. These are the things shard *proves work over real WAN* that your brief underweighted.

1. **Async ring pipelining (the 2.94 → 16.6 tok/s win) — HIGHEST VALUE.** `coordinate_pipe()` in `specpipe.py:434-511`. Your brief assumed **synchronous one-ring-traversal-per-step** — that is the 2.94 tok/s regime and it is the single biggest thing you got wrong. The fix: keep `depth` verify-chunks in flight simultaneously, speculatively chaining each chunk on its *own* draft output (`dprefix = dprefix + ds; send_pos += K`) without waiting for verification. Per-token cost collapses from *full ring latency* to *per-stage throughput*. On divergence, set `discard = len(inflight)` and drain-skip the stale results — greedy acceptance keeps output identical. **Portability: high** — pure scheduling logic, maps cleanly to your `FuturesUnordered`/async worker model. **Difficulty: medium.** The KV bookkeeping (`StaticKV` writes at absolute `start`, fresh chunk overwrites stale KV "for free") is the subtle part. **This is the headline lift.**

2. **Direct-return ring.** `specpipe.py:664-669` (`ret_sock` + `{op: hello_return}` handshake), non-tail stages run `direct=True` and become forward-only (skip the `if not direct: send_msg(conn, recv_msg(nxt_sock))` relay), tail uses `select` over two connections to disambiguate. Tail → coordinator in **one hop** instead of N hops back up the chain. **Required** for pipelining to pay off (otherwise the return path serializes and doubles RTT). **Portability: high** — your QUIC stack already does bi-streams; this is a routing topology, not new transport. **Difficulty: low-medium.** Your router already brokers connections, so the rendezvous/handshake fits your model.

3. **CUDA-graphed draft with static-KV speculative rollback.** `glm_swarm_nvfp4_cg.py`: `_WRITE_POS` fixed-address scalar tensor for the write slot, `cg_causal_mask` built from `position_ids` (not max-written length) so a rewound position auto-masks the stale tail, `cudagraph_mark_step_begin()` to stop buffer reuse, 8-step warmup. 3.8× draft speedup in isolation (49.7 → 13.1 ms/tok). **Portability: LOW and this is the honest caveat.** Every primitive is HF-transformers + torch-specific; llama.cpp has its own CUDA-graph support but the `_WRITE_POS`/`create_causal_mask`-monkeypatch design is meaningless outside torch. **AND shard hasn't even landed it** — it's "cudagraph-speed XOR clean-rollback," the diff harness `glm_swarm_nvfp4_cg_diff.py` exists because rollback still corrupts the static path. **Difficulty: high, value uncertain.** Recommendation: **don't port this yet.** Start with llama.cpp's eager draft + physical KV crop (their working 16.6 tok/s `DynamicCache` baseline uses `dcache.crop(pos)`). The CG-draft win is a *later* optimization and llama.cpp's KV API is different enough that you'd design your own version anyway.

4. **Pickle-free authenticated wire.** `phase0/wire.py`: JSON header + length-prefixed raw LE tensor blobs, dtype whitelist (`_DTYPES`), ChaCha20-Poly1305 per-frame (fresh 96-bit nonce, key from `SHARD_PSK`), `InvalidTag`/parse-error → `ConnectionError` so edge supervision resets. **Portability: you're already ahead.** DKN runs Quinn 0.11 + rustls challenge-response keyed to node identity — that's shard's *Phase-1 target* (per-node identity over QUIC-TLS), which they haven't built. **Lift the idea, not the code:** the load-bearing insight is the **quantized activation codec** (`ActivationCodec`, fp8/int8 — still a stub in shard, but the rationale "activations tolerate quantization far better than weights, bandwidth is the cost center on a home uplink" is correct and is what makes WAN pipelining viable). **Difficulty: low** for the codec on top of your existing msgpack framing.

5. **Verifiable receipts.** `phase0/proof_receipt.py` + `docs/PROOF.md`: four skeptic-checkable proofs (distinct public IPs / distinct GPU UUIDs / multi-region; min edge RTT > 1ms = real WAN; output SHA256; reference-match). **Portability: conceptual.** This is a per-*run* "was this genuinely distributed and reproducible" artifact — orthogonal to DKN's per-*node* `validate_prefill` logprob-stride scheme. Worth adopting the *output-hash + WAN-RTT* receipt for the pipeline path because **your single-node validation doesn't cover multi-party pipelines** (see §4). **Note their own defect:** `build()` doesn't emit `engine_sha256` and `verify()` never checks it, despite PROOF.md calling it the primary anchor — don't copy that gap.

6. **Topology solver (bonus — it's the one fully-implemented file).** `topology.py`: min-latency Hamiltonian loop with coordinator as depot, Held-Karp exact for n≤16, NN+2-opt above, optimizing on **measured asymmetric RTT** (`c_out` = coord→head, `c_in` = tail→coord, `L` = stage-to-stage). For N=6-10 shards this is squarely in the exact-Held-Karp regime. **Portability: trivial** — it's ~100 lines of pure algorithm, Apache-2.0, directly translatable to Rust. **Lift this verbatim.** Your router already measures RTT for failover; this turns that into optimal shard ordering.

## 4. UPDATED ARCHITECTURE DELTA — `docs/pipeline-specdecode-design.md`

Concrete changes to your prior brief:

- **DROP the synchronous one-traversal-per-step model.** This is the central revision. Replace with the **async depth-N pipelined ring** (technique #1). Add `pipeline_depth` as a config knob. Re-baseline expectations: synchronous is ~3 tok/s over WAN; pipelined is ~16 tok/s — a 5× difference that changes whether the product is viable at all. Your design's latency budget section is now wrong and must be rewritten around per-stage throughput, not round-trip latency.
- **DROP the MTP-head requirement and the vocab-mismatch warning.** Per §1: GLM-4-9B greedy-drafts GLM-5.2 directly via exact token-id acceptance. Keep MTP only as a one-line fallback note ("if measured `g` with GLM-4-9B < ~0.5, evaluate the checkpoint's depth-1 MTP head").
- **ADD direct-return as a hard requirement, not an option.** Document the two-channel tail (`ret_sock` + forward-only intermediate stages). Wire it through your router's connection brokering.
- **KEEP the custom llama.cpp cut — but stage it.** Phase 1: eager draft + physical KV crop (shard's *working* `DynamicCache` path), greedy-only, async-pipelined ring. Defer the CUDA-graph static-KV-rollback draft (technique #3) to a later phase — it's high-risk, torch-specific, and *unfinished even in shard*. Don't put it on the critical path.
- **ADD a multi-party validation gap to the open-problems section.** Your existing `validate_prefill`/`TrustScorer` validates a *single node ran the claimed model*. A pipeline output is the *joint* product of N nodes; a middle node can forward plausible garbage. Shard's M7 ("random redundant recompute, spot-check a block on a trusted node, activation commitments + slashing") is *exactly* the pipeline generalization of your stride-logprob recompute — and they have no answer. **This is now your hardest unsolved problem too,** and your existing redundant-recompute primitive is the most promising starting point in either codebase.
- **ADD the activation-privacy decision.** Shard flags that a malicious mid-pipeline node reconstructs **35-59%** of user tokens from activations. Their mitigation is **boundary pinning** (embed + final layers on trusted/staked nodes, untrusted volunteers hold deep middle only) tied to stake. DKN doesn't face this today (whole models per node), but pipeline-parallel introduces it. Decide now: boundary-pin to staked nodes, route "trusted-only" per request, or disclose honestly. This is a product decision, not an engineering one.
- **ADD the activation codec** (fp8/int8 quantized hidden states) to the transport layer — bandwidth is the cost center on home uplinks.

Keep watching their M7 work — whoever solves trustless multi-party pipeline verification first has the real moat, and you have a head start with your existing validation rails.

**Build custom on llama.cpp/Rust/QUIC as planned — your core instinct was right — but with three corrections shard proves are non-negotiable: (1) your GLM-4-9B-drafts-GLM-5.2 plan is valid, so drop the MTP detour and the vocab warning; (2) replace synchronous one-traversal-per-step with shard's async depth-N pipelined ring plus direct-return, the difference between ~3 and ~16 tok/s and the make-or-break for product viability; (3) defer the CUDA-graph static-KV-rollback draft — it's torch-specific and *unfinished even in shard* — and ship the eager-draft + physical-KV-crop path first. Do not adopt the engine — it inverts DKN's heterogeneous-reach moat.**

**Single most valuable de-risking experiment:** Stand up a **3-node hidden-state pipeline cut of a mid-size GGUF MoE (or dense ~30-70B) on llama.cpp over real WAN — NVFP4 not required — and measure GLM-4-9B-style greedy spec-decode acceptance rate `g` AND the synchronous-vs-async-pipelined throughput delta.** This single test resolves the two load-bearing unknowns shard can't answer for *your* substrate: (a) does exact-token-id greedy acceptance actually yield a usable `g` with llama.cpp's quantized logits (shard proved it for NVFP4-on-Blackwell; you need it for GGUF K-quants, where per-engine rounding differs), and (b) does the 5× pipelining win hold over llama.cpp's per-stage decode throughput. If `g` is decent and the async delta materializes, the whole architecture is de-risked and the only remaining hard problem is multi-party validation (§4). If `g` collapses on GGUF quants, you learn *that* before building the ring — and that's the one failure mode neither your brief nor shard's Blackwell numbers can rule out for you.
